tested in CI, but not yet exercised on real hardware or with real data. **⏳ partial**: some of it works and the

## Playback

| Feature | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Playback startup diagnostics | 🧪 | Web development builds record per-attempt startup milestones without media URLs or credentials (#210); Reel Web for Android runs the same web app. The Compose app's Media3 instrumentation was removed with it (#260). Synthetic comparison procedure is in `scripts/startup-benchmark/README.md`. Real-device baselines remain pending |
| Playback decision engine | ✅ | Chooses direct play, remux, audio-only transcode or full transcode from the device profile and the file's streams. Reasons appear in Admin → Now playing |
| Hardware transcoding (VAAPI on the N100) | ✅ | Full GPU pipeline (decode → scale_vaapi → h264_vaapi). Measured at 8.3× realtime for 1080p→720p. Falls back to software per attempt |
| Low-power VAAPI encoders (#113, part 1) | 🧪 | VOD transcodes use the fixed-function encoder (`-low_power 1`) when its startup test encode passes, separately for H.264 and HEVC. A low-power failure before the first output retries the same session with the regular VAAPI encoder, then software. Admin → Hardware lists the low-power codecs; Admin → Now playing shows each transcode's encoder (e.g. `h264_vaapi (low power)`). `REEL_VAAPI_LOW_POWER=0` turns it off. Live TV keeps the regular encoder. HEVC output for HEVC-capable clients (part 2) waits on #111 |
| Keyframe-accurate HLS for remux | ✅ | Reads the keyframe index from MKV Cues / MP4 `stss` (kilobytes, not the whole file). Segments are validated before serving, so seek/resume stays stable |
| Compressed HLS playlists | 🧪 | Every `.m3u8` (VOD master and media playlists, live, IPTV VOD) goes out brotli or gzip when the client sends `Accept-Encoding`. A 2.5 h film's signed media playlist: 216 KB → 6.5 KB gzip / 2.8 KB br, so hls.js reaches its first segment request sooner on slow links. Segments, direct play and other media are never compressed and keep `Range` (#112) |
| Adaptive quality (server) | ✅ | `quality` = auto/original/1080p/720p/480p plus a `max_bitrate_kbps` hint. Ladder: 8/4/2 Mbps, never upscales |
| Quality choice (Settings → Playback → Quality per device; quality sheet in the player) | 🧪 | Auto / Original / 1080p / 720p / 480p, sent as `quality` on every library and IPTV VOD play (#87). The player's sheet re-decides from the playhead and shows each choice's bitrate and why one isn't offered (your bitrate limit, no smaller than the original). "Original" never re-encodes the picture silently: when the device or a bitrate limit forces it, the player says why and offers **Play transcoded**. Stream info says direct play / remux / transcode and why in one line. Casts use the server's Auto |
| Adaptive quality (player: bandwidth estimate, automatic step-down/up) | 🧪 | #111 ②–③, #1. In browsers that play HLS with hls.js (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Android), the web player asks for a multi-variant session: the original plus up to three lower rungs in the same codec family, each transcoded only while it's fetched. hls.js switches between them per segment. The player measures the connection from the segments it downloads (and a short probe during direct play), keeps the estimate per server on the device for 3 days, and sends it on decide: it picks the start variant and whether a direct-playable file stays direct play (estimate ≥ 1.5× its bitrate) or is remuxed with rungs. The quality picker switches without a new session: Auto lets hls.js choose, Original pins the original, and 1080p/720p/480p cap it at that size. A choice the session doesn't list (Original on a title that has to be converted, or a rung left out) reopens the session as before. Two stalls in 30 s during direct play under Auto switch to the adaptive session from the playhead. Stream info shows the playing version, the list of versions and the measured speed; Settings → Playback shows the measured speed. Safari, webOS/Tizen, casts and IPTV VOD keep one rendition (Q5/Q6), and `REEL_ABR=0` turns it off for everyone. Built and tested; checked against the live server over a slow link (stayed on 480p, local pin to Original), not yet verified by the owner |
| Subtitles | ✅ | Reel's own overlay on every platform. Text subtitles → WebVTT. Image subtitles are burned in. Human-readable labels such as "English · SDH" |
| Automatic audio/subtitle selection | ✅ | Settings → Playback (languages, Off / Forced only / Automatic / Always, SDH). The server picks the tracks and explains why |
| Playback recovery (web) | 🧪 | hls.js plays HLS in every MSE browser, Chrome included (Chrome's own HLS player gave up on the first bad segment). VOD keeps ~90 s buffered with longer segment timeouts, so bitrate peaks don't drain it. Network errors, MSE/decoder errors and stalls of 25 s (live: 12 s) are retried by themselves (reload at the playhead or the live edge, `recoverMediaError`, `swapAudioCodec`, rebuild) with a **"Reconnecting…"** overlay. After 8 failed attempts the player reopens the session (live: re-tunes) once, at most once a minute, before it shows the error card (#91, #93). Safari keeps its native HLS player; TV apps use their own players. **Pause is kept** (#100): a recovery or an automatic reopen never starts a paused video. **Long pauses** (#95): a paused player keeps pinging, and the server keeps a paused converted session for 30 min instead of 60 s. When the server has ended the session anyway (reaped, restarted), the player opens a new one at the same position, right away if it was playing and on the next play if paused. A stall's error now says whether a segment request is stuck in flight or none was made (#96) |
| Player UI | ✅ | Redesigned controls: centred transport, scrubber with a time tooltip, labelled actions, volume/speed/PiP, keyboard shortcuts, D-pad seek mode |
| Click on the picture (web) | 🧪 | Decided by the pointer type, as on YouTube. **Mouse:** a click plays or pauses at once, with the centre play/pause flash; a double-click toggles full screen and undoes that click, so playback ends where it started. **Touch or pen:** a tap shows the controls, or hides them if they're up, and never plays or pauses; a double-tap on the left or right third seeks −10 s / +10 s with a "−10 s / +10 s" ripple on that side, and further taps there add up (−20 s, −30 s…); a double-tap in the centre toggles full screen. **TV mode** (remote, magic-remote pointer): unchanged, a click shows/hides the controls and OK/Space pause. **Live TV** can't pause, so in full screen a click or tap shows/hides the overlay and a double-click leaves full screen (#167, #183) |
| Player menus and toasts in full screen (web) | 🧪 | Speed, audio, subtitle and "Play on…" sheets, and toasts, mount inside the full-screen player, so they open over the video and close back to it without leaving full screen. Before, they opened invisibly behind it (#94). Every sheet (quality, speed, audio, subtitles, "Play on…", and the other pickers that share it) and the Live TV mini guide have a **✕** in the header: click it, or reach it with ↑ on the D-pad and press OK. Focus goes back to the button that opened the menu; Back/Escape and clicking outside still close it (#168) |
| Next episode in full screen (web) | 🧪 | *Next* (⏭) in full screen keeps full screen: the route's wrapper (`div.player`) is the full-screen element and stays mounted while only the episode changes, so the new episode opens full screen and, in Reel Web for Android, the phone stays in landscape. There's no automatic play of the next episode yet. Checked with Playwright in Chrome (desktop mouse, phone touch, 1920×1080 TV mode with arrow keys; mock mode and the live API) and on the Android 15 phone emulator in Reel Web with real taps (#247) |
